Patterns

Fifteen years of finding the shape in messy data — mostly customer behaviour, mostly at scale, mostly under a deadline.

  • Predicting churn before anyone had a bad feeling

    Renewal risk was being flagged by account managers on instinct, usually about three weeks too late to act on. The signals were all sitting in product telemetry, support history, and billing — just never in the same place.

    Built a gradient-boosted model over a unified feature store, scored the full book weekly, and pushed the top risk drivers back into the tools the team already used rather than a separate dashboard.

    Risk visible a full quarter earlier, with a named reason attached to every flag.

    Python · Snowflake · dbt · Tableau

  • A health score people actually trusted

    The previous score was a weighted average nobody could explain, so nobody used it. Trust was the real problem, not accuracy.

    Rebuilt it as two legible dimensions instead of one opaque number, with every input traceable to a definition the business had signed off on. Shipped a simulator so stakeholders could move the weights themselves and see what happened.

    Adoption went from occasional to default, largely because people could argue with it.

    Python · Excel · Snowflake

  • One definition of “active customer”

    Four teams, four numbers, every meeting spent reconciling them instead of deciding anything.

    Moved core metrics into a governed semantic layer so the definition lived in one place and every downstream report inherited it. Unglamorous work; the highest-leverage thing on the roadmap.

    Reconciliation meetings stopped happening.

    dbt · Snowflake · Tableau
